\section{Prototyping}\label{sec:prototyping}
This section discusses our experience building  using Flutter and React Native.


\subsection{Flutter}
Setting up Flutter was, in our opinion, the most challenging part.
Adding the PATH manually and configuring the Dart SDK in Android Studio is well documented, but it’s easy to make small mistakes that can take hours to troubleshoot.

Working with widgets felt fairly straightforward and provided structure quickly.
Built-in components such as the Scaffold allowed us to lay out pages fast, which made it satisfying to see the interface come together early in development.
However, whenever we wanted to move beyond the default Material UI, we often had to write a significant amount of additional code, and styling pages sometimes became tricky.
Each widget approaches styling slightly differently, and parameter names are not always consistent, which was frustrating.

On the positive side, the Flutter ecosystem is very strong.
Adding functionality like content sharing was simple and required minimal code.
Many useful features, such as RefreshIndicator and Snackbar, are available out of the box, so we didn’t need many extra dependencies.

The documentation was generally helpful, although we occasionally spent time searching for specific widgets when we didn’t know their exact names.
The emulator provided mixed results: it is resource-intensive and sometimes displayed the app differently than a real device, but switching to wired debugging on a physical phone resolved most of those issues.

For the prototype, we used Android Studio as our IDE.
We implemented shared preferences for local storage and integrated the camera and path provider packages to capture images and store their paths on the device.
Overall, once we became familiar with the ecosystem and workflow, Flutter made the development process approachable, and we appreciated how it simplified tasks that can be more tedious in other frameworks.
